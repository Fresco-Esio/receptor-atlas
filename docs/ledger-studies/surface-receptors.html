Neuronal surface receptors

A neurotransmitter is only a key. What it does depends on the lock: the same dopamine, serotonin or glutamate molecule opens a channel in a millisecond, starts a second-messenger cascade lasting minutes, or, through neurotrophin receptors, changes gene expression over hours. This tutorial walks through the three receptor families that sit on the neuronal surface, with working models you can operate.

Three families, three timescales

Every cell surface receptor converts an extracellular signal into an intracellular one. The families differ in how many steps lie between binding and effect, and each step adds both delay and amplification.

Ligand-gated ion channels

Binding site and pore are one protein. Effect is a change in membrane conductance.

No amplification. Speed: ~1–100s ms.

G-protein–coupled receptors

Seven transmembrane helices. The receptor is a guanine-nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) for a heterotrimeric G protein.

Amplifies at several steps. Speed: ~100 ms to minutes.

Receptor tyrosine kinases

Single-pass receptors that dimerize and cross-phosphorylate. In neurons: the Trk family for neurotrophins.

Kinase cascades to transcription. Speed: minutes to days.

Family 1

Ligand-gated ion channels

The receptor is the channel. Agonist binding to the extracellular domain shifts the transmembrane helices that line the pore, the gate opens, and ions move down their electrochemical gradients. Whether the result excites or inhibits depends entirely on which ions pass and where their reversal potential sits relative to threshold.

Excitatory or inhibitory is set by the reversal potential

A channel drives the membrane toward the reversal potential (Erev) of the ions it passes. Cation channels (nAChR, AMPA, NMDA, 5-HT3) have Erev near 0 mV, well above threshold, so they depolarize. Cl⁻ channels (GABA-A, glycine) have Erev near ECl, at or below rest in mature neurons, so they hyperpolarize or shunt.

NMDA receptor: a coincidence detector

operable model

I–V curve: g(V) = 1 / (1 + [Mg²⁺]/3.57 mM · e−0.062·V), the Jahr & Stevens (1990) fit, × driving force (Erev ≈ 0 mV). Dashed line: Mg²⁺-free.

−70 mV

Try: glutamate on at −70 mV (almost no current), then slide toward −20 mV. That is the postsynaptic depolarization from coincident AMPA input. Ca²⁺ entering through the relieved channel is the trigger for LTP. Then add ketamine with the channel open, and turn the agonists off: ketamine stays trapped in the closed channel (use-dependent, trapping block). Voltage-dependent Mg²⁺ block: Nowak et al. (1984), Mayer et al. (1984). Glycine requirement: Johnson & Ascher (1987).

Other ionotropic receptors worth knowing cold

nAChR. Muscle type is (α1)₂β1δε in adults (γ replaces ε in fetal muscle). Neuronal α4β2 is the main high-affinity nicotine site and the target of varenicline (partial agonist). Homomeric α7 is highly Ca²⁺-permeable.

AMPA. Carries most fast excitatory current. Receptors containing Q/R-edited GluA2 are Ca²⁺-impermeable; GluA2-lacking receptors pass Ca²⁺. LTP expression involves inserting more AMPA receptors into the synapse. Perampanel is a non-competitive antagonist; topiramate antagonizes AMPA/kainate receptors among its actions.

Glycine receptor. Cl⁻ channel mediating inhibition in spinal cord and brainstem. Strychnine blocks it. GLRA1 mutations cause hyperekplexia.

5-HT3. The only ionotropic serotonin receptor (all others are GPCRs). Ondansetron blocks it; mirtazapine and vortioxetine also antagonize it, which is why they cause less nausea than SSRIs.

Family 2

G-protein–coupled receptors

A GPCR has no pore and no enzyme activity of its own. Agonist binding shifts transmembrane helices (especially TM6) outward, opening an intracellular pocket that the G protein's α subunit docks into. The receptor then acts as a guanine-nucleotide exchange factor: it pries GDP off Gα, GTP (abundant in cytoplasm) binds, and the heterotrimer splits into Gα-GTP and Gβγ, each of which regulates effectors. One receptor activates many G proteins before it is shut off, which is the first point of amplification.

Turning the signal off

GTP hydrolysis. Gα has intrinsic GTPase activity; RGS proteins (regulators of G-protein signaling) accelerate it. Gα-GDP then reassociates with Gβγ. This sets the duration of each G-protein activation.

Homologous desensitization. G-protein receptor kinases (GRKs) phosphorylate the agonist-occupied receptor's intracellular loops and tail. β-arrestin binds the phosphorylated receptor, sterically blocks further G-protein coupling, and recruits clathrin for internalization. Arrestins can also scaffold their own signaling (for example ERK), the basis of "biased agonism."

Why it matters clinically. Desensitization and internalization underlie tolerance to opioids and other agonists. The delayed onset of SSRIs has been attributed in part to gradual desensitization of somatodendritic 5-HT1A autoreceptors in the raphe; that is a long-standing hypothesis rather than an established mechanism.

Lithium and the inositol cycle

IP₃ is recycled to free inositol by inositol polyphosphate 1-phosphatase and inositol monophosphatase (IMPase), and inositol is needed to rebuild PIP₂. Lithium inhibits both enzymes. Berridge, Downes & Hanley (1989) proposed that this depletes inositol preferentially in neurons with the highest Gq activity, dampening overactive signaling. Lithium also inhibits GSK-3β directly. Neither mechanism is established as the therapeutic one.

Family 3

Receptor tyrosine kinases: the Trk family

Neurotrophins are dimeric growth factors. Each neurotrophin dimer bridges two Trk receptor monomers, bringing their intracellular kinase domains together so that each phosphorylates tyrosines on the other (trans-autophosphorylation). Phosphotyrosines become docking sites for adapter proteins that launch three cascades. These signals regulate survival, dendritic growth, spine formation and synaptic plasticity, mostly through changes in transcription and translation, which is why they act over hours.

BDNF Val66Met

The common Val66Met polymorphism (rs6265) sits in the BDNF prodomain. In Egan et al. (2003), Met-BDNF showed reduced activity-dependent secretion with normal constitutive secretion and failed to localize to secretory granules and synapses. In humans, the Met allele was associated with poorer episodic memory and abnormal hippocampal activation on fMRI.

Ketamine: from an ion channel to a kinase (hypothesis)

One widely cited model links the families on this page. NMDA blockade (preferentially on tonically active GABAergic interneurons) disinhibits pyramidal neurons, producing a glutamate burst that activates AMPA receptors. BDNF is released, TrkB signals through mTORC1, and new spine synapses form in prefrontal cortex. In rats, Li et al. (2010) showed ketamine rapidly activated mTOR and increased spine synapses, and blocking mTOR with rapamycin abolished both the synaptogenesis and the antidepressant-like behavior. Competing accounts (direct GluN2B effects, eEF2 kinase, the metabolite hydroxynorketamine) remain debated.

Clinical correlates

Where receptor mechanisms turn into PRITE vignettes.

Anti-NMDA-receptor encephalitis ionotropic

IgG against the extracellular N-terminal domain of GluN1. Antibodies reduce surface NMDA receptors and synaptic clusters, an effect reversed by antibody removal. In Dalmau's 2008 series of 100 patients: median age 23, 91% women, 59% with tumors (most often ovarian teratoma). All had psychiatric symptoms or memory problems; most then developed seizures, dyskinesias, autonomic instability and hypoventilation.

Myasthenia gravis vs Lambert–Eaton ionotropic

MG: antibodies against the muscle nAChR (postsynaptic ligand-gated channel). Fatigable weakness, ptosis, diplopia.

LEMS: antibodies against presynaptic P/Q-type voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels, not a ligand-gated receptor. Associated with small-cell lung cancer; strength transiently improves with repeated effort.

Strychnine, tetanus, hyperekplexia ionotropic

All three remove glycinergic inhibition. Strychnine blocks the glycine receptor; tetanus toxin cleaves synaptobrevin and blocks glycine and GABA release from spinal inhibitory interneurons; GLRA1 mutations cause hereditary hyperekplexia (exaggerated startle, neonatal stiffness), which responds to clonazepam.

Benzodiazepine vs barbiturate overdose ionotropic

Benzodiazepines only modulate GABA's effect, so there is a ceiling. Barbiturates at high concentrations open the channel directly, so there is no ceiling, and combinations with alcohol or other depressants are dangerous. Flumazenil reverses BZD-site effects only and can precipitate seizures in dependent patients or in TCA co-ingestion.

Neurosteroids for postpartum depression ionotropic

Brexanolone (IV allopregnanolone, approved 2019) and zuranolone (oral, approved 2023) are GABA-A positive allosteric modulators acting at synaptic and extrasynaptic δ-containing receptors, which benzodiazepines do not modulate.

Bacterial toxins Gs Gi/o

Cholera toxin ADP-ribosylates Gαs, blocking GTP hydrolysis, so cAMP stays high. Pertussis toxin ADP-ribosylates Gαi/o, uncoupling it from receptors, so inhibition of adenylyl cyclase is lost and cAMP also rises. Pertussis-toxin sensitivity is the experimental test for Gi/o coupling.

Lithium: inositol and the kidney Gq Gs

Brain: inhibits IMPase and inositol polyphosphate 1-phosphatase, limiting PIP₂ regeneration for Gq signaling (Berridge, 1989); also inhibits GSK-3β. Kidney: lithium enters collecting-duct principal cells through ENaC and reduces aquaporin-2 expression downstream of the V2 (Gs) receptor, causing nephrogenic diabetes insipidus. Amiloride blocks ENaC entry.

5-HT2B agonism and valvulopathy Gq

5-HT2B agonists cause fibrotic valvular heart disease: fenfluramine (the fen-phen era) and the ergot-derived dopamine agonists pergolide and, at high doses, cabergoline. Relevant when cabergoline is used for antipsychotic-induced hyperprolactinemia, though doses there are low.

Xanomeline–trospium M1 M4

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist without D2-blocking activity. Trospium is a peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist added to blunt peripheral cholinergic effects. In the phase 2 EMERGENT-1 trial, PANSS total fell 17.4 vs 5.9 points with placebo at 5 weeks; common adverse events were constipation, nausea, dry mouth, dyspepsia and vomiting (Brannan et al., 2021). FDA-approved for schizophrenia in September 2024.

Pitolisant and the H3 autoreceptor Gi/o

H3 receptors are Gi/o-coupled presynaptic autoreceptors that limit histamine release. Pitolisant, an H3 antagonist/inverse agonist, removes that brake and is approved for excessive daytime sleepiness and cataplexy in narcolepsy. Contrast with H1 antagonism (Gq), which sedates.

α2 agonists Gi/o

Clonidine, guanfacine, lofexidine and dexmedetomidine activate α2A receptors. Somatodendritic and presynaptic α2 receptors on locus coeruleus neurons reduce firing and NE release, the basis for treating opioid withdrawal (lofexidine), ADHD (guanfacine, clonidine ER) and acute agitation (sublingual dexmedetomidine). Postsynaptic α2A in prefrontal cortex is thought to underlie guanfacine's cognitive effects.

BDNF Val66Met TrkB

Met allele: reduced activity-dependent BDNF secretion with normal constitutive secretion, poorer episodic memory, abnormal hippocampal activation (Egan et al., 2003). A frequently tested example of a functional polymorphism in a neurotrophin pathway.
